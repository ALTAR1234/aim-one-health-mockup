Early Diagnosis

How Multidisciplinary Deliberation Can Reduce Diagnostic Delays

Finding specialists isn't the hard part. Getting the right ones together early is.

Introduction

With a serious diagnosis, much of the time goes not to treatment but to working out what information treatment needs. Symptoms or an abnormal scan lead to a string of consults, tests and referrals.

Each step has a purpose, but together they can feel scattered, with repeat tests, delays and stress. Patients often ask one thing:

"Why couldn't we have known this sooner?"

The Reality of Diagnostic Delays

Take a newly found lung mass:

  • Imaging shows a suspicious lesion.
  • The patient is referred to a specialist.
  • A biopsy is done.
  • Pathology confirms cancer.
  • An oncologist then asks for molecular testing.
  • More genetic markers are requested.
  • More imaging is ordered for staging.
  • Treatment talks wait until everything is back.

Each request is sound, but they're found one after another instead of up front. The result: several rounds of tests, weeks between visits, delayed planning, repeat consults and unclear next steps. For families, the waiting can be as hard as the diagnosis.

Why Information Gaps Create Delays

Treatment plans in complex disease draw on many fields:

  • Radiology
  • Pathology
  • Laboratory medicine
  • Genetics and molecular diagnostics
  • Surgical specialists
  • Medical specialists
  • Care coordination teams

Asked one at a time, some questions only come up late:

  • Should molecular testing have come earlier?
  • Is more pathology work needed?
  • Are biomarkers needed before choosing treatment?
  • Would more imaging change the plan?
  • Should other diagnoses be checked?

Late questions usually mean another round of testing.

The Cost to Patients

Delays hurt more than the schedule. Patients often feel:

  • Uncertain: something is wrong, but the next step isn't clear.
  • Frustrated: each visit raises new questions.
  • Overwhelmed: advice comes from many doctors with no single view.
  • Anxious: every week feels like time lost.

Many patients call the diagnostic phase one of the most stressful parts of their care.

A Different Approach: Earlier Multidisciplinary Deliberation

Reviewing the case from several angles at once surfaces information needs at the start, not one specialist at a time. It can point to:

  • High-value diagnostic tests.
  • Biomarker requirements.
  • Genetic and molecular testing needs.
  • Extra pathology considerations.
  • Missing clinical information.
  • Possible treatment implications.

The point isn't to skip steps. It's to know which steps matter most before delays build up.

Better Prepared Before Treatment Decisions

"Diagnostic readiness" means having what's needed for an informed decision before the key conversation. With a fuller picture, clinicians can:

  • Discuss suitable treatment options.
  • Explain risks and benefits.
  • Answer patients' questions.
  • Cut unnecessary test rounds.
  • Coordinate care better.

And patients see their path more clearly.

The Future of Diagnostic Decision-Making

As care gets more specialized and data-heavy, bringing experts together early matters more. The aim isn't just faster diagnoses but better-informed ones: the right tests sooner, fewer wasted cycles and experts aligned around the patient. Every delay avoided counts.

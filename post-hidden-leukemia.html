Diagnosing a Hidden Leukemia in the Emergency Department

A synthetic patient followed from ED triage to remission by a grounded multi-agent AI reasoning system, with every recommendation kept auditable.

An APL patient can look like someone with a mild virus, and missing it can be fatal within days. Caught early and treated with ATRA plus arsenic trioxide, it's usually curable. The study asks whether a multidisciplinary AI system can follow the whole journey, from triage to remission, while showing its reasoning at every step.

Abstract

  • Background: APL with DIC is an emergency where recognition at triage is the error-prone step. The study tested whether the BrainMAAS reasoning system could handle the full care arc auditably.
  • Methods: One synthetic scenario across five stages. A council of seven specialist agents, a router, a consensus layer and a citation rule reasoned over live sources (ClinicalTrials.gov, PubMed, ChEMBL, ICD-10). A single-model baseline without retrieval was the comparison.
  • Results: Belief in APL rose 0.25 → 0.98, with a deliberate dip when other causes became plausible. ATRA was started on strong suspicion before genetic confirmation. Both arms reached the diagnosis at stage 2; the system did better on calibration, safety flags, citations and trial matches.
  • Conclusions: The scaffold didn't make diagnosis more accurate, but it made the reasoning grounded, safety-checked and easy to follow. Wider validation is needed before drawing clinical conclusions.

1 · Introduction

Prognosis in APL depends on recognizing it quickly and starting differentiation therapy before the genetic test comes back. The report states each claim as a clinical hypothesis and checks it against what the simulation actually produced, with methods and limits laid out so others can scrutinize and reproduce it.

2 · Methods

2.1 Scenario and study design

One synthetic scenario in five stages: triage, first lab results, deterioration, the critical treatment decision, and disposition. With a single case, the goal is to show how the system behaves, not to measure accuracy across a population.

2.2 System architecture (BrainMAAS)

Four layers: a live, provenance-hashed evidence store; a router that picks which specialists lead at each stage (Emergency Medicine at triage, Hematology-Oncology after labs); seven specialist agents reasoning in parallel; and a ranking layer that builds the plan under a citation rule. Every therapy suggestion needs a source and a confidence level, and any claim that can't be grounded must be declared rather than invented.

2.3 Comparator

The same model, called once per stage with the same clinical picture but no retrieval, no councils and no citation rule, isolating what the scaffold adds.

2.4 Molecular constraint analysis

ESMC-600M scored every single-residue change across the 246-residue RARα ligand-binding domain (4,920 estimates) to find the most mutation-intolerant positions. A 3D structure prediction was attempted but not completed because of a network limit.

2.5 Endpoints

The belief trajectory, treatment-plan quality (0–5), calibration (1 − Brier score), recall on a six-item safety checklist, routing precision, citation coverage and recruiting-trial matches.

3 · Results

3.1 Diagnostic trajectory

Confidence rose for reasons visible at each stage. The key move came at stage 3, when ATRA was started before the genetics confirmed it. Confidence dipped slightly at deterioration because other causes of bleeding became plausible, then recovered with the molecular result.

3.5 Molecular constraint on the drug target

The most mutation-intolerant positions sit in the ligand pocket and helical core of RARα, the receptor ATRA re-activates. The drug relies on the most conserved part of its target, one reason the treatment works so reliably.

3.6 Clinical course

Vital signs were most unstable at deterioration, with a fever bump during induction, then normalized. Labs show the DIC crisis, its correction, and molecular clearance of PML-RARA by consolidation. Heart rhythm (QTc) was watched throughout.

3.7 The full care arc and trial matching

After stabilization: induction, consolidation, maintenance, molecular remission and survivorship, each with its own monitoring. Live matching ranked recruiting trials; the top fit was a Phase 3 ATRA-combination trial (NCT07504458). Survivorship covers heart, bone, fertility and emotional support.

3.8 Engineering course-corrections

  • Giving each council stage-specific information fixed an early run that stalled at about 0.85.
  • Per-request limits and a fallback fixed empty answers at the final stage.
  • An audit caught a chart plotting design assumptions instead of logged values; it was corrected.
  • One claim couldn't be grounded, so the system said so instead of inventing a citation: 208 of 209 grounded.

4 · Limitations

This is one synthetic case. It shows how the system reasons but can't establish accuracy or superiority. Other caveats: idealized synthetic data, scoring against the simulation's own outputs, no external validation or second rater, a computational (not lab) molecular analysis, and retrieval from a single point in time.

5 · Conclusions

The system followed the case from triage to survivorship, started treatment at the right moment, tracked safety, and matched trials, with every recommendation traceable. It didn't out-diagnose the single model; its value was grounding, safety, calibration and navigation, including admitting the one claim it couldn't support.
